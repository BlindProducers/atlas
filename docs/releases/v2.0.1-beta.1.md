Hey everyone! This beta is mostly about the Sample type, which now has a Slicer mode and time stretching, and it also brings plenty of smaller fixes and speech improvements. I’d love to hear how it works for you, so please let me know if anything sounds or reads wrong.

**Highlights**

- **Slicer mode:** the Sample type can cut a file into up to 128 slices and play one per key, and the new Slices editor lets you move, split, pitch, reverse and play each slice from the keyboard.
- **Time stretching:** a sample can follow the host’s tempo or keep its own length on every key, so a loop stays in time when you play it higher or lower.
- **More Sample controls:** Reverse, Normalize and Loop shift, loop points and the root key taken from the file, and files up to two minutes long.
- **64 voices** instead of 32.
- **Copy and paste** LFOs, envelopes and random modulators from their headings’ menus.
- **Clearer speech:** the LFO and Trance gate editors say the point or step number first, and pitch, phase and voice count values now say their units.
- **Fixes** for SFZ instruments with keyswitches, wavetable harmonic editing, the Convolution Reverb’s predelay and more.

A big thank you to Matthew Whitaker for several of the speech fixes in this release!

## What changed since 2.0.0.5

### Sample type

- **Slicer mode.** The Sample controls now start with two buttons, Classic, the sampler as it was, and Slicer. Slicer cuts the file into slices, either on an even grid (Number of Slices) or at its transients (Sensitivity), and plays slice 1 on the root key and one more slice per semitone up. Each slice plays to its end, on to the end of the file with Play Through, or repeats with Slice Loop. Stretching is set to Tempo by default, so a loop cut on the grid lands on the beat.
- **Slices editor.** Up and Down move between slices, Left and Right move a slice’s start by 10 ms (1 ms with Shift), Return splits a slice in the middle, Backspace or Delete removes it, Space plays it, and R moves every start to a random place. Comma and Period change a slice’s pitch, Page Up and Page Down its gain, V turns it around and 0 puts all three back. Every edit can be undone.
- **Stutter, Stutter Chance and Slice Decay.** Stutter repeats the start of a slice 2 to 16 times within the slice’s own length, so the groove stays on the grid, and Stutter Chance sets how often that happens. Slice Decay fades each slice out as it plays, which helps tighten up drum loops.
- **Time stretching.** Stretch Mode keeps a sample’s pitch and length apart. Tempo fits the sample’s beats, at Sample BPM, to the host’s tempo, and Fixed keeps the file’s own length on every key, scaled by Stretch Length (25% to 400%). Preserve Formants keeps the file’s formants in place when the pitch moves. Sample BPM is taken from a WAV’s ACID data, or from a tempo in the file name such as “120bpm”.
- **Reverse, Normalize and Loop shift.** Reverse plays the file backwards and can be modulated, Normalize plays the file with its loudest peak at 0 dB, and Loop shift slides the whole loop through the file, which works nicely with an LFO or a macro. Loop points set the wrong way round now loop backwards.
- **Loop and root key from the file.** Loading a WAV or AIFF takes its loop and root note when it has them, or the root note from a note in the file name, such as “Piano C3”.
- **Start, End and the loop points in your units.** Their context menu has Show as, with Percent, Milliseconds and Samples, and the arrow keys step in that unit. This replaces the Fine tune toggle.
- **Longer files.** Sample, Granular and Spectral now keep up to two minutes of a file, instead of about 40 seconds.

A patch that uses the Slicer, stretching or the new switches won’t play the same in 2.0.0.5 or earlier, since those versions don’t have these controls.

### Modulation and editors

- **Copy and paste LFOs, envelopes and random modulators.** The menu on each one’s heading now has Paste to and Copy from, as oscillators already did, and a paste can be undone in one step.
- **Flip an LFO shape.** In the LFO editor, F turns the shape upside down and Shift F plays it backwards.
- **Bend a curve from the keyboard.** In the LFO editor, Command Up and Down (Ctrl on Windows) bend the segment after the point in 5% steps, or 1% with Shift.
- **The LFO and Trance gate editors say where you are first.** The LFO editor starts with the point number, such as “Point 2 of 5”, and the Trance gate editor reads the step number and says “not played” for a step past the end of the pattern.
- **Trance gate keys.** Up and Down now change a step’s shape, instead of G and H. Ramp up now rises from silence to the step’s level, the opposite of Ramp down, and older presets keep sounding as they did.
- **Shift D is gone from the LFO editor.** It copied a shape over the next LFO with no way to undo it. Use Copy from on the LFO’s heading menu instead.
- **Granular shows only the rows its settings use.**
- **Up to 64 voices.** Polyphony now goes up to 64, and saved presets keep their setting.

### Speech and keyboard

- Pitch, phase and voice count values say their units, for example “2 semitones”, “90 degrees” and “1 voice”, and the EQ’s Resonance reads in percent on every band.
- Modulation slots 21 to 64 are named in words, like the first twenty.
- The Keyboard help starts with the shortcuts that work everywhere, and on the Mac it says Option instead of Alt.
- The preset filter lists’ help reads “select a tag” and “select an author”.
- The Sample and Granular root keys read their note, such as “C sharp 4, MIDI 61”, instead of always “C”.

### Fixed

- **SFZ instruments with a keyswitch range now play.** Instruments such as UI Metal GTX made no sound, because every key in their keyswitch range was treated as a keyswitch.
- **Editing one harmonic leaves the others alone.** In the wavetable editor, raising or lowering a harmonic used to change the level of every other harmonic too. Keyframe, line and interpolation edits can now be undone, and the frame count updates after a frame is removed.
- **A wavetable file takes its file’s name.** A table loaded from a file used to keep the name stored inside it, so a resynthesized table read “Preset resynthesis” wherever it was loaded.
- **Convolution Reverb’s predelay.** With some predelay settings, the reverb could keep replaying its impulse response even with the host stopped, and changing the predelay could make a burst of noise.
- **Short loops keep their shape.** A single-cycle loop played above or below its root key could pick up clicks or change its tone.
- **Round Robin returns to the key you pressed last.** With one voice, releasing a key now goes back to the most recent key you’re still holding.
- **Shared and exported patches no longer include file paths from your computer.**
- **Atlas opens and loads faster,** especially on Windows, where NVDA could go quiet for a moment while the editor opened.
