## What changed since beta 1

### New

- **Speech oscillator.** A new oscillator type that sings the words you type, with the voice of eSpeak NG. Type the words into the slot's text box and press Enter: each note sings the next syllable, the notes of a chord sing one syllable together, and the words rewind when playback starts. Its controls:
  - **Mode.** Sung sings each note's pitch and holds the vowel while the key is down. Spoken keeps eSpeak NG's own intonation and plays the words straight through, with middle C at the voice's own pitch and other notes moving the whole contour.
  - **Split.** Each note takes the next syllable, the next word or the whole text.
  - **Language and Variant.** Menus over eSpeak NG's 150 languages and 104 voice variants, such as other speakers or the Klatt synthesizer.
  - **Formant** moves the voice's formants up or down in semitones without changing the pitch. **Breathiness** blends in a whisper of the same words, up to a full whisper. **Speed** sets how fast the consonants and unheld vowels play, 0% to 400%. **Vibrato Depth** and **Vibrato Rate** add a vibrato that fades in after each note.
  - **Amplitude Envelope.** Voice lets each syllable shape its note and keeps the voice open until its last consonant; Envelope 1 shapes the voice with Envelope 1, as the other oscillator types do.
  - Transpose, Tune, FM and ring modulation from other oscillators work as on the other types. FM bends the pitch at audio rate while the formants and the syllable's timing stay put.
  - While the host plays, the syllables follow the song position: starting playback or moving the playhead anywhere sings the syllable a play from the beginning would, loops included, and hosts that chase held notes are handled. A chord with notes added while a key is down keeps singing one syllable until every key is up. In mono Legato mode a note change keeps its syllable at the new pitch.
  - The words are saved in presets; where in the song the notes start is saved with the host's project.
  - The plug-in bundles now carry eSpeak NG's data (about 29 MB), so the installers and zips are larger than beta 1's.
- **Maim effect.** Maim by Arden Butterfield, in the Distortion and character category: the sound goes through a real codec and back, with Maim's circuit bends on the MP3 encoders. Its controls:
  - **Encoder.** Blade and LAME are the MP3 encoders, each with its own tone; Opus is the internet codec; GSM the 13 kilobit telephone codec; LPC a 2.4 kilobit vocoder, with its own **LPC Sample Rate** from 2 to 22.05 kHz.
  - **Bitrate** for MP3 and Opus, **Turbo**, **Tilt**, **Error** and **Mix** at the top of the slot.
  - For the MP3 encoders, Maim's bends in groups: Spectral (**Frequency Shift**, **Level Shift**, **Feedback**), Butterfly (**Straight**, **Crossed**), and MDCT (**Band Step**, **Band Invert** and **Window Step** for LAME; **Band Shift** and **Band Reverse** in place of Maim's band reassignment matrix).
  - For Opus, GSM and LPC, a Packet loss group: **Loss Period**, **Loss Width**, **Loss Jitter** and **Lost Packets** (Drop or Stick).
  - **Drive**, **Low Cut**, **High Cut** and **Makeup** in the Gain and filters group.
  - Changing the encoder or bitrate crossfades to the new one; the host is told the codec's latency, which follows the encoder. Each codec runs at its own rate behind a resampler, so the effect sounds the same whatever the oversampling.
- **Particles effect.** A granular texture processor, in the Space category, built on Mutable Instruments' granular processor (MIT). It records what comes in into a buffer and plays it back as overlapping grains. Its controls:
  - **Mode.** Granular sows grains from the buffer; Pitch shifter and time stretcher plays it back continuously, splicing where the waveform matches; Looping delay replays it as a delay that loops and pitch shifts; Spectral freezes and reshapes its spectrum.
  - **Quality** sets how much sound the buffer holds: about 1 second in 16-bit stereo, 2 in 16-bit mono, 4 in 8-bit stereo and 7 in 8-bit mono, the 8-bit settings sounding grainier.
  - **Input Gain** (−18 to +6 dB), **Position**, **Size**, **Pitch** (±48 semitones), **Density** (negative sows grains at a steady rate, positive at random, 0 none), **Texture**, **Mix**, **Stereo Spread**, **Feedback**, **Reverb**, **Freeze** and **Trigger**. What Position, Size, Density and Texture do changes with the mode, and each one's description says how.
  - Every note played fires a grain, and so does Trigger, an Off/On slider, each time it turns on (modulation turns it on above 50%), so with Density at 0 grains play only when triggered.
  - Every slider, and Freeze, can be modulated from its context menu.
- **Saturator, Dynamic Tube and Overdrive effects**, in the Distortion and character category, and **Multiband Dynamics**, in Dynamics: native ports of Alessio Scala's JSFX, which recreate the Live devices of those names from measurements. The DSP is the scripts' own, checked sample for sample against them, and every control keeps its script's range and units.
  - **Saturator.** Eight curves (Analog Clip, Soft Sine, Bass Shaper, Medium Curve, Hard Curve, Sinoid Fold, Digital Clip and Waveshaper), Drive, Color filters before and after the curve, Post Clip, Output, Pre-DC Filter, Hi-Quality 2x oversampling and Dry/Wet. Bass Shaper Threshold shows with the Bass Shaper curve and the Waveshaper group with the Waveshaper.
  - **Dynamic Tube.** Tubes A, B and C, Drive, Bias, Envelope with Attack and Release, Tone, Output, Hi-Quality and Dry/Wet.
  - **Overdrive.** A band-pass filter (Frequency and Width) into a clipper, with Drive, Tone, Dynamics and Dry/Wet.
  - **Multiband Dynamics.** Low, Mid and High band groups, each with Active, Solo, Input Gain, Above and Below thresholds and ratios (read as 1 : x), Attack, Release and Output Gain, below the Low Band and High Band switches and the two splits; Detection (RMS or Peak), Soft Knee, Time, Output and Amount in a Global group. Twelve factory FX presets come with it, from the script's own: Standard Multiband Comp, OTT, Multiband Gate, Split Band De-esser and more. The script's sidechain is not included.
  - Hi-Quality adds 3 samples of latency to Saturator and 4 to Dynamic Tube, and Overdrive's look-ahead adds 8 at 2x oversampling; the host is told, and the dry signal is delayed to match. Every slider can be modulated from its context menu.
- **Arpeggiator rates can be modulated.** Free Rate, Sync Rate and Time Mode are modulation destinations now. Every step the arpeggiator plays is a new note, so they take only sources that run on their own: LFOs, random LFOs, macros, the mod wheel and pitch bend. Assigning another source says why it is refused.
- **Multi-sample loads sample files of any length.** DecentSampler instruments that slice one long recording (Claustrophobic Piano's is over 134 million frames) loaded none of their samples. The fixed cap is gone; the memory budget still applies.

- **Community presets in the preset browser.** The Collection list has a Community presets row beside All presets and Favourites. It lists the patches shared on [atlas.blindproducers.org](https://atlas.blindproducers.org), the community preset library, with the uploader as the bank and the style as the category, so the Library, Bank, Category and Tags lists and the search narrow them as they do the presets on disk. Return on a row downloads the preset into the Community library (`Community/Presets/<uploader>/<style>` in the Atlas folder), checks it against the library's checksum and loads it; from then on it shows among all presets too, and the Downloaded column says which ones are here. The list is fetched in the background when the row is chosen and kept for ten minutes; Refresh preset list fetches it again. A preset not downloaded yet is never autoloaded while scrolling. While a preset or a bank downloads, a Download progress bar and a Cancel download button appear after the Autoload toggle, below the preset list: the bar's name carries the percentage that has arrived, said again every couple of seconds while the bar or the button has the focus, and Cancel download stops the download, removes what had arrived and puts the focus back on the preset list. A download is announced with its name and size, as in Downloading CoolPiano, 1.1 GB, and an upload the same way.
- **Community preset previews.** A shared preset whose uploader attached a recording says so in its details, with the recording's length. P on the row, or Play preview in its context menu, fetches the recording into `Community/Previews` in the Atlas folder the first time and plays it through Atlas's output beside whatever is playing; P again stops it, starting another row's preview stops the first, and Atlas says when a preview starts, stops and finishes.
- **Community banks.** The Collection list's fourth row, Community banks, lists the banks shared on the library, with the uploader as the bank and the genre as the category; each row's details say what the bank holds (24 presets, 3 wavetables and 2 samples), how large the download and the unpacked files are, and whether it is imported. Return downloads the bank's archive into `Community/Banks/<uploader>` in the Atlas folder, checks it against the library's checksum and unpacks it as Import preset bank does, so its presets then show among all presets under the bank's name; Return on an imported bank unpacks it again. A bank's preview plays with P like a preset's.
- **Share patches and banks on the community library.** The Preset menu has Share patch on the community library and Share bank on the community library. While Atlas is not logged in, either opens a login window first: it says that no API token was found, takes your personal API token in a box that hides what you type from the screen and the screen reader, and has a button that opens the API tokens page of your account on [atlas.blindproducers.org](https://atlas.blindproducers.org) in your browser. Log in checks the token with the library and keeps it in the system's password store, never in a file, a preset or the host's project. On macOS that is your login keychain, where each DAW keeps its own: you log in once in each DAW, and the keychain never asks for your password. On Windows it is Credential Manager, on this computer only, and one login serves every DAW. Where the store will not keep it, Atlas holds the token in memory until the DAW closes, and says so. Then the form opens in the editor, like Save patch: for a patch, its name, style, tags and description, filled in from the patch, with the library's styles to choose from (Atmosphere, Bass, Bells, Brass, Chords, Drums, Experiment, Guitar, Keys, Lead, Organ, Pad, Percussion, Piano, Pluck, Sequence, SFX, Strings, Template, Vocal, Woodwind, World and Other); for a bank, the folder of the user library to share (or another folder), its name, genre, tags and description. Share uploads in the background: the focus moves onto a progress bar that reads the percentage sent, said again every couple of seconds while the bar keeps the focus, and the form's Cancel, named Cancel upload while the upload is on and next after the bar, or Escape stops the upload. Atlas then says "Shared ... on atlas.blindproducers.org", or the library's reasons for refusing it, field by field, with the form left open to fix them. A token the library refuses, deleted on the site say, brings the login window back, and the share goes on once a new one is accepted. Settings logs out. Escape or Cancel closes the form and the focus goes back where it was.
- **Settings.** The Editor menu is gone: its button is now Settings, which opens the Settings window at once. Everything the menu held is in that window, as a group VoiceOver enters (VO Shift Down) and NVDA names as the focus moves in, while Tab moves through every control in turn. Plugin info shows the version, with the beta (Atlas 2.0.0 beta 2, VST3 plug-in), and has buttons that open GitHub to report an issue, read the source code and see the releases. Undo and redo are buttons named after what they would do, for hosts that keep Command Z for themselves. Tuning says which tuning is loaded, loads a tuning file and clears it. MIDI learn mappings lists every mapping with a button that removes it, and Remove all. Accessibility holds the speech settings the old window had. Online community browser turns the community library on or off in the preset browser and the Preset menu (on by default; off, Atlas makes no network requests for it and the audio thread does no extra work), says which account the saved API token belongs to, how much it has shared and where the token is kept, logs in with a token and out again, and opens atlas.blindproducers.org. Keyboard help holds every shortcut in a text box read line by line. The window scrolls when it is taller than the screen, and Escape closes it from anywhere in it, the text boxes too.

### Fixed

- **The editor is called Atlas.** Screen readers read "Keyboard and VoiceOver accessible editor for Atlas" after the editor's name; they now read Atlas alone.
- **Rename macro and the Move to Trash question.** A macro's context menu showed a garbled character after Rename macro, and on macOS the question before moving a preset to the Trash showed garbled quotes around the preset's name. Both read plainly now.
- **Export folder as bank over an existing bank.** Exporting onto a bank file that was already there added the new archive to the end of the old file, which then would not import. The archive is now written beside it and replaces it once complete.
- **Mod wheel and pitch bend on global parameters.** A route from a wheel to a parameter outside the voices, such as an effect's mix or the volume, moved only while a note played and stayed where the last voice left it. It now follows the wheel whether or not a note is playing.
- **VoiceOver stays on the control after prompts, dialogs and rebuilds.** Typing a value, adding modulation, loading a wavetable or an instrument, or showing the wavetable editor could leave VoiceOver at the top of the window. The screen reader now returns to the control it was on, or its replacement when the rows were rebuilt, and announcements made meanwhile wait for that move instead of being cut off. Escape closes the value prompt.
- **EQ, Mid Side EQ and Compressor rows are grouped by band.** Each EQ band has its own group (Low band, Band 1 to 4, High band; Mid Side EQ puts them in a Mid and a Side group). The compressor keeps Enabled, Enabled Bands, Attack, Release and Mix at the top of the slot, with Low, Mid and High band groups below, each saying when its band runs.
- **Windows build.** This is the first beta whose Windows build includes the Speech oscillator; linking eSpeak NG no longer puts its compatibility headers ahead of the system headers.

### Building from source

`git submodule update --init` now also fetches `third_party/espeak-ng`, which the build compiles together with its language data, and `third_party/opus` for the Maim effect. Maim's circuit-bent LAME and BladeEnc and libgsm are kept in the repository. eSpeak NG is GPL v3 or later, Opus BSD-3, LAME and BladeEnc LGPL, libgsm its own permissive licence; see `NOTICE` and `third_party/README.atlas.md`.
